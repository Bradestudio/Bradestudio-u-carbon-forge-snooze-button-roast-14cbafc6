## The entry (141 characters)

> "My alarm rang at 6 AM on a Saturday like it had a shift to clock into — mate, the only job here is ruining the one morning I actually earned."

## Why it works

The alarm isn't a hated object here — it's an overeager employee clocking into a Saturday shift it was never assigned. That fresh angle flips the usual "I hate mornings" complaint: the alarm has the work ethic, and the human is the one who earned the morning off. The concrete image (a shift to clock into) lands the joke, and the punchline pays off in the last few words — "the one morning I actually earned."

## How it works

`main.py` (Python stdlib only, no network, no clock) gate-checks the line (1–200 chars, single line, no leading/trailing whitespace) and writes it — with no trailing newline — to `entry.txt` inside `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (fallback `/output`). Deterministic and byte-identical across runs.
